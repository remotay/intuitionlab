The Memory Ladder

Everything your processor touches lives somewhere on a ladder — caches millimetres from the core, memory centimetres away, disks an eternity below. Each dot here crosses its track in exactly one access time. Slow the clock until you can watch the cache sprint — and the disk stand still.

Why the ladder exists

In one nanosecond, light itself travels only 30 centimetres. A cache that answers in a nanosecond must sit millimetres from the core — and it does. Every rung below is bigger, cheaper, and farther away: physics sells capacity or speed, never both at once.

What a miss costs

A modern core can retire several instructions per cycle. In the ~90 ns it waits for DDR5 it could have executed a thousand; during one disk seek, tens of millions. This is why processors predict and prefetch — and why fast software keeps its working set near the top of the ladder.

Typical, not gospel

Real latencies vary with the chip, the queue depth, and plain luck — read every figure here as an honest order of magnitude. The shape is the story: each rung is 3× to 300× the one above, and from L1 to a hard disk the gap is eight million to one.
