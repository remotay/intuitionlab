The Bandwidth Race

The Memory Ladder asked how long the first byte takes. Bandwidth asks a different question: once the data flows, how wide is the river? The gap

One second of Blackwell Ultra is a minute and a half of desktop DDR5.

Two different questions

Latency is how fast memory answers; bandwidth is how much it can carry. A van full of tape drives has dreadful latency and enormous bandwidth. The ladder and the race measure the same hardware — and rank it differently.

Why the GPU wins

Bandwidth is bus width times clock. A DDR5 DIMM talks over 64 wires per channel, across centimetres of motherboard. Apple solders LPDDR5X onto the package and makes the bus many times wider. GDDR7 pushes each of a graphics card’s 512 wires to extreme speed. And HBM3e stacks the memory dies right beside the GPU with thousands of connections — that is how you feed tens of thousands of cores.

Typical, not gospel

Figures are peak, spec-sheet rates: dual-channel DDR5-5600 at ~90 GB/s, an M3 Ultra Mac Studio at 819 GB/s, an RTX 5090 at 1,792 GB/s of GDDR7, a Blackwell Ultra at ~8,000 GB/s of HBM3e. Real workloads land lower — but the ratios hold, and they are why modern AI lives and dies by memory bandwidth.
