The Light Clock

A photon bouncing between two mirrors is the most honest clock there is: one strike, one tick. Set an identical clock moving and watch — from your seat — its ticks fall behind. Nothing here is broken. This is time dilation.

Why the path slants

Both clocks are identical: two mirrors, one photon, one tick per strike. But Clock B’s mirrors slide sideways while its photon is in flight, so from where you stand the photon must cut a diagonal to keep meeting them — and a diagonal between the same two mirrors is simply a longer trip.

Why the ticks slow

Light travels at c for every observer — it cannot speed up to make up the difference. A longer path at the same speed takes more time, so each tick of Clock B stretches by γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²). At 0.866 c the clock ticks half as fast; at 0.99 c, a seventh.

It isn’t the clock — it’s time

Muons born 10 km up should decay long before reaching the ground; moving near c, their inner clocks run slow, and they arrive. GPS clocks are corrected daily for the same effect. And aboard Clock B the photon just bounces straight up and down — from there, it is your clock that lags. Both views are right. That is relativity.
